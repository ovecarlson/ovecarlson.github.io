about digital work


Inspired by Sol LeWitt´s “Paragraphs on Conceptual Art” (1967), the idea of a physical machine that could generate artwork had been within me for a long time. It resulted in my designing of a software that was implemented by Simon Carlson, a master's student at the Royal Institute of Technology in Stockholm.

Through custom-developed software I explore the intersection of geometry, color, chance, and algorithmic processes. I create systems capable of generating an unlimited number of visual compositions from simple geometric elements such as lines, circles, and rectangles. Rather than producing individual images directly, I design the conditions from which images emerge. By defining parameters for form, color, transparency, scale, and layering, I establish a framework within which controlled randomness can operate. Each generated image is unique, shaped by a dynamic relationship between predetermined structure and unpredictable variation.

A central aspect of my work is the shift from creating singular objects to creating systems of possibility. The artwork exists not only in the images that appear but also in the underlying process that continuously generates them. In some works, images are selected by me and presented as fixed outcomes. In others, the software operates autonomously, producing an endless sequence of transient images (ARTOMATIC) that appear briefly before disappearing and being replaced by new ones. This approach challenges traditional notions of authorship, permanence, and originality.

The artist becomes both creator and system designer, while the artwork unfolds as an ongoing dialogue between intention and emergence. No viewer encounters exactly the same work, and no image occupies a privileged position within the larger generative field.

My work is informed by the traditions of geometric abstraction, conceptual art, and generative art, while engaging with contemporary questions about algorithms, complexity, and perception. Ultimately, I am interested in how simple rules can produce unexpected visual richness, and how an artwork can exist not as a fixed object, but as a living process of continuous transformation.
